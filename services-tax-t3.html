Trust and Estate Tax Returns
Handled with Care

Professional T3 trust returns and estate tax filing, including final returns for a deceased taxpayer.

Trusts and estates have their own tax filing rules, and mistakes can be costly for trustees and executors. Herb Wodehouse Professional Corporation prepares T3 trust returns and estate returns, and helps you meet your filing obligations with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA).

We prepare returns using TaxPrep, including T3 returns, slips to beneficiaries and, for a deceased taxpayer, the final T1 return. We file electronically with the CRA.

Our Trust & Estate Process

01 Initial Review

We review the will or trust document, the assets and the key dates, and confirm what must be filed and by when.

02 Information Gathering

We collect statements, valuations at date of death, and income and expense records for the trust or estate.

03 Return Preparation

We prepare the T3 return, the T3 slips for beneficiaries, and any final or prior-year T1 returns required.

We file with the CRA and help you request a clearance certificate before assets are distributed.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • When is the T3 return due?
    A T3 return is generally due 90 days after the end of the trust's tax year. For trusts with a December 31 year-end, that is March 31.
  • Does an estate have to file a T3 return?
    Usually yes, if the estate earns income after the date of death or has other filing triggers. We will confirm what applies to your situation.
  • What is a final return for a deceased person?
    It is the T1 return covering January 1 to the date of death. It is due April 30 of the following year if death occurred from January to October, or six months after death if it occurred in November or December.
  • What is a clearance certificate and why does it matter?
    It is a CRA confirmation that all tax owing has been paid. Executors who distribute assets without one can be personally liable for unpaid tax, so we recommend requesting it.
  • Are there special trust reporting rules?
    Yes. Many trusts must also report additional information, such as details of trustees, settlors and beneficiaries. We will tell you which schedules apply.
  • I am an executor. Where do I start?
    Contact us. We will review the will, the assets and the key dates, and set out what must be filed and when.
